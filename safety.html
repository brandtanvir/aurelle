(01)Who operates your flight

Aurelle arranges your flight. A certified operator flies it, and we tell you which one before you pay.

An aircraft flown for hire must be operated by a company that holds an air operator certificate for that kind of flying: a certificate with Part 135 authority from the Federal Aviation Administration in the United States, an Air Operator Certificate issued under EASA rules in Europe and Switzerland, or one from the UK Civil Aviation Authority in Britain. The operator holds operational control. It is responsible for the aircraft, the crew and the safe conduct of every flight.

Aurelle Aviation SA arranges your flight as your agent. The aircraft is flown by one of our three affiliated operators, based in Geneva, Farnborough and Teterboro, or by our partner carrier in Dubai. Each holds its own air operator certificate, and your quote and charter agreement always name:

  • the operating carrier’s legal name, certificate number and issuing authority;
  • the aircraft type and its registration;
  • the audit ratings that operator holds today.

The full statement of our role and the carriers we work with is in our Operator disclosure.

(02)How we choose operators

We book an operator only after it passes every one of these checks, and we check again every year.

  • A valid certificate and operations specifications for the aircraft type and the region flown.
  • At least one current independent audit rating (see the next section).
  • Third-party liability insurance of at least USD 300 million, combined single limit, with Aurelle named as an additional insured.
  • A working Safety Management System that meets ICAO Annex 19.
  • No regulatory enforcement action affecting safety in the last five years.
  • Aircraft maintained under an approved programme, with records reviewed twice a year by our Director of Technical Operations.
  • A full review after any incident or serious occurrence, before we book the operator again.

If an operator falls below any of these standards, we stop booking it. We do not make exceptions for price or availability.

(04)Crew standards

Two pilots on every flight, both trained on the exact aircraft type, both employed by an operator we have audited.

  • Two pilots on every flight, including light jets that may legally be flown by one.
  • Captains hold an Airline Transport Pilot licence, with at least 5,000 total hours and 1,000 hours on type.
  • First officers hold a type rating, with at least 2,500 total hours.
  • Simulator training at least every six months at an approved training centre.
  • Flight and duty time limits set by the operator’s authority, plus our own rule: at least 12 hours’ rest before every duty.
  • Background screening and a signed confidentiality agreement before a first flight with Aurelle guests.
  • Cabin hosts trained in emergency procedures and first aid, and certified in food hygiene.

(05)Before and during every flight

  • A trip risk assessment covering weather, airports, route and crew rest.
  • An independent check of the trip, crew and aircraft before departure, through ARGUS TripCHEQ.
  • Airport suitability: runway, approach, opening hours and terminal security.
  • Weather, de-icing and airspace notices reviewed again on the day.
  • Remote medical advice in flight from emergency physicians on call 24 hours a day, and a defibrillator on every aircraft.
  • Baggage checked against ICAO rules on dangerous goods.

The captain has the final word on whether, when and where a flight operates. We will never ask a crew to fly outside their limits, and we will tell you plainly when weather or safety changes a plan.

(06)Discretion and security

Crews, handlers and our own team sign confidentiality agreements. We share your details only with those who need them to operate the flight: the operator, the handling agent at each airport, and the border and customs authorities that require advance passenger information by law.

We never publish or sell passenger lists, and we do not confirm to anyone that you have flown with us. Our Privacy notice explains exactly what we keep and for how long.

(07)Reporting a concern

Anyone, whether a passenger, crew member or member of the public, can report a safety concern to safety@aurelle.example. Reports reach our Head of Safety directly, are acknowledged within 24 hours and can be made anonymously.

The operators we work with report occurrences to their aviation authority as the law requires. We review every report that involves an Aurelle flight and act on what we learn.
