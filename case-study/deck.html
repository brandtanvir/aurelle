AurelleThe brief

(1.1)What was asked

“They don’t show too much. But whatever they show really works for the customer.”

The clienton Apple, Nike and American Express as the benchmark

Jesko Jets

Cinematic, scroll-led storytelling and one aircraft at a time.

“Let’s Fly” concept

Airy minimalism: huge type, one image, almost nothing else.

Apple product pages

Restraint: one idea per screen, motion that explains.

AurelleThe brief

(1.2)The real problem

Three tensions every decision had to resolve.

Spectacle × speed

Immersive 3D and film on a phone, without ever feeling slow. Spectacle lives in a few scenes; everything else is type and space.

Mystery × price

“If you have to ask” loses the aspirant buyer. Indicative estimates, clearly labelled, never a firm promise.

Beauty × function

Every beautiful part does a job: the globe plans routes, the fleet compares aircraft, membership calculates.

AurelleThe brief

(2.4)Insights → decisions

Six things we learned, and what each one changed.

InsightDecision
The principal judges brand level in seconds, often on a phoneOne headline and one image above the fold; the phone hero is designed, not shrunk
Assistants need the shortest route to a human“Book a flight” in the nav at every scroll position; phone number in menu and footer
Price opacity reads as exclusive but loses the aspirantIndicative estimates in the planner and booking; real prices on empty legs
Privacy matters more than featuresDiscretion is the first chapter; the form asks for the minimum
Visitors skew older, on high-end screens, in bright lightLight-first design, AA contrast, large tap targets, nothing hover-only

AurelleChapter three

(03)Chapter

Strategy

Decide what to leave out before deciding what to put in.

AurelleStrategy

(3.1)We added

Six things that do a job.

  • +A membership whose hourly rate falls the more you fly
  • +An empty-legs board: the one place price can be shown openly
  • +A route planner inside the 3D globe
  • +A four-step quote in about sixty seconds, no account
  • +A named concierge with a stated ten-minute reply
  • +Discretion as a feature: NDAs, private terminals

(3.2)We left out, on purpose

Six that only add noise.

  • −Accounts and dashboards
  • −Instant card payment
  • −Blog and press-logo walls
  • −Champagne stock photography
  • −Chatbots and pop-ups
  • −Testimonials (they break discretion)
AurelleStrategy

(3.3)Principles

  1. 01One idea per screenIf a section needs two headlines, it is two sections.
  2. 02Silence is a materialWhitespace is budgeted, never what is left over.
  3. 03Motion explainsEvery animation reveals hierarchy, space or cause and effect.
  4. 04Beauty that worksThe globe plans, the fleet compares, membership calculates.
  5. 05Under-promiseNo exclamation marks. No stacked superlatives.

Aurelle05 · Iterations

(05)Feedback → response

Six times the client said no. Six better answers.

#FeedbackResponse
01“Use a serif. No, sans serif.”DM Sans everywhere, self-hosted; type carries the luxury, not the font category
02“Drop the dark version, make it sky and white.”Light only; navy kept for scenes that need depth (fleet, globe, footer)
03“No italics, no blue in the text.”Black + grey two-tone headings; blue reserved for data and focus
04“The cabin window looks fake and illustrative.”Four full-screen chapters of real footage and cabin photography
05“The black jet kills the vibe on navy.”Same shape, re-rendered in pearl white with a texture-remap shader
06“The globe doesn’t feel flagship.”NASA night Earth, atmosphere, light trail and city labels on deep navy
